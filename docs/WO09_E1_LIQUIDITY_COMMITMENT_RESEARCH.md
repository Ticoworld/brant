# Track D — what liquidity is actually purchased

Model evidence is TESTED SYNTHETIC, informed by real prior A2 lock/gate and commitment proofs. No new native service-level or third-party identity proof follows from those old transactions; inherited counts are excluded from WO09 results.

A contract can fund I units of inventory, restrict early owner withdrawal and define a price/limit. It cannot guarantee more than I cumulative execution without a funded replenishment obligation. After legitimate consumption, zero depth is not an inventory-conservation failure. A public-depth-for-duration promise also fails if the maker can self-consume through controlled counterparties. A lock and a useful independent service are different promises.

The compensation grid varies executed quantity, adverse price change, explicit operating cost and issuer retainer. Profit is retainer minus selection loss minus actual costs (plus only genuinely earned executed spread/fees). Unrelated P2P principal netting does not pay an LP. At zero outside volume, a retainer can still pay an availability service, but it comes from a real issuer/treasury budget, not free volume income.

Alternatives compared: ordinary professional MM contract, firm RFQ, owner-timelocked A2, exact-outpoint group cancellation, allocated execution quota, competing backstops and funded strike/protection rights. Revocation is active and ordering dependent, not an upper quote expiry. Oracle-based repricing adds publisher/freshness/liveness assumptions; a keeper cannot guarantee immediate cancellation.

Correction to the strongest failing promise: replace unlimited persistent third-party depth with explicit consumable quota/firm optionality. Four250-unit funded quotas exactly allocate1000 inventory. This resembles an option or inventory auction; it may be rational if purchasers/issuers pay for it, but it is not a novel automatic-yield market. Penalties require separate posted collateral and a contractually observable breach; independent-customer identity is not supplied by blockchain addresses.

Decision: STOP SPECIFIC MECHANISM for an unfunded unlimited-depth/automatic-fee promise. REVISE finite paid capacity as a possible component of a funded rights market. Actual issuer willingness to pay, maker risk premium, replenishment capital, monitoring/oracle fees and enforceable third-party service are unknown. Do not rebuild the old timelock as a new product or count its prior transactions as WO09 proof.

## Independent retainer and capital-cost investigation

The new exact alternative model includes inventory, term, annual opportunity cost, volume, adverse move, executed spread and service costs. Minimum retainer=max(0, idle/term carry + adverse selection + operations - actually earned spread). Positive quoted interest at an unexecuted spread pays nothing. Some synthetic cases need more than half the inventory amount as retainer; others are cheaper. A real issuer budget and maker reservation quote, not a blockchain balance, decides whether the service is rational.

WHAT IF the service is a fixed allocated inventory option rather than persistent public depth? It has a truthful finite limit and a payer, but resembles existing firm RFQ/funded optionality. WHAT IF a treasury prepays availability at zero volume? It may be coherent, but the payment consumes real issuer finance. Third-party customer identity and objectively enforceable service breach remain unresolved. No new native SLA or retainer mandate is claimed.
