## Candidate investigation records

**A — Service reservation.** Prospective provider class: CKB data/API operators such as Cellora; buyer class: independent dApp developers needing a defined allocation. Provider's actual funding amount, immediate cash fraction and price are **unknown**. Credible benefit: a provider-authorized quantity, time window and priority reservation, if they will legally/operationally honor it. Transfer matters only when another eligible developer wants the unused allocation; no such buyer, bid or provider permission has been found. Price must reflect remaining service, provider failure risk, fallback cash, maturity, fees and alternatives. No LP is presumed. Archive/proof operation must be separately priced. If provider disappears, backed cash can remain recoverable under qualified chain conditions; service itself cannot be compelled. A cash-plus-service arrangement only wins if scarce commitment, discount or cancellation protection is valuable enough to offset locking cash. None is yet measured.

**B — Launch participation.** Ohrex supplies a concrete competing token-launch effort ([public introduction](https://talk.nervos.org/t/ohrex-protocol-decentralized-token-launchpad-with-auto-dex-deployment-on-ckb/10162), S12), not a named issuer asking to sell a protected Trade package. No token with established buyer utility, issuer financing requirement or real bid was found for our proposed bundle. Token identity can be authenticated; future usefulness, legal profit rights and revenue are separate. Transfer price depends on both funded redemption and genuinely demanded upside. No automatically willing LP or issuer buyback exists. Creator disappearance leaves only genuinely funded rights/assets, not promised business performance.

**C — Milestone financing.** Published grant requests establish real staged-finance jobs and governance alternatives. The grant payer/treasury values public deliverables, not necessarily resale rights. Exact immediate needs differ: CrowdCell asks for a completed-work tranche; Cellula pays for setup; Kirea proposes an initial fraction. Do not replace these with a universal 20% rule. Quality acceptance needs buyers/reviewers or another explicitly trusted process. If creator disappears, unreleased escrow can refund; spent money cannot be returned without another capital source. CrowdCell and existing grant committees may already solve the narrower job more simply.

**D — Earned merchant balances / operator-funded liquidity.** Hosted payment/mint operators and their merchants have a stronger reason to need withdrawal rights: money has already been earned, not gratuitously advanced for a cash claim. Potential buyers are service purchasers, then merchants become cash creditors. Retaining the purchaser's cash would mean not buying the service. However independent real merchant balances, desired protection fee and frequency are unknown. Existing Fiber self-custody may be a better solution. A separate LSP-capital job has a named capital provider in S09, but no request for Trade leasing; active channel capital cannot simultaneously remain a liquid reserve in our vault. These are financial-market hypotheses, not an instruction to build another indexer or payment platform.

Across all four: no executable secondary bid, buyer price premium, service-resale permission, archive SLA, conversion quote or Trade-specific paid order was obtained. Public grant support is evidence of spending on development, **not** evidence people want to buy this financing structure.
