## Final participant and open-discovery check

Legitimate issuer financing and speculative creator launches share accounting constraints but different customer value: the first needs usable development funds, the second cannot substitute speculative volume for independent demand. An early buyer's discount/bonus is paid by issuer proceeds, existing surplus or another explicit payer. A later buyer owes a real bid; no prototype guarantees one. An LP/residual maker contributes actual missing assets and carries selection/inventory/replenishment risk; principal netting pays no service income. A treasury may pay a bounded availability retainer even at zero volume, but no such mandate was observed.

For term rights, the senior supplies protected-side principal, junior supplies loss-absorbing principal, publisher supplies a maturity observation and a secondary buyer supplies immediate exit cash. Both parties fund buffers/fees and lock until actual maturity or an agreed fallback. The publisher/relayer needs explicit service revenue; zero test compensation is not a business assumption. The senior risks oracle error and loss beyond the funded protection cap; junior risks total principal loss. Secondary buyer takes those rights/risks for an agreed price. Each party can decline if cash/spot/options is cheaper.

Independent open-ended discovery investigated selling an existing settlement entitlement rather than launching another token or selling its backing twice. The native result is a **consumable beneficiary right**: owner-authorized sale without the other counterparty, followed by unilateral settlement and return of occupied record capacity. This might generalize to funded receivables, redemption queues, access or inventory claims, but those are future hypotheses, not newly executed products. Strongest counterargument: the right is economically option/claim prior art, needs a real buyer and authenticated event, and increases peak state. No distinct customer need is established merely by expressiveness of the Cell Model.
