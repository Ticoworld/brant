## Required price/ticket stress cases

All rows face hypothetical$0.50/$1/$2/$10CKB, tickets$1/$5/$20. Native asset/financial rights conserve actual unit amounts regardless of dollar price, but meaningful dollar protection requires an asset/price source and actual counterparty. Fixed per-owner receiving capacity becomes more expensive in dollars with CKB appreciation. Shared storage amortizes while principal and exit liquidity do not disappear. Fixed-term rights also tie up collateral for their full term and require a paid risk writer. Paid commitments add inventory duration/opportunity cost; they cannot deliver more assets than remain funded. Exact numerical shared/direct scenarios are in `WO09_TRADING_LIQUIDITY_ECONOMICS.md` and reproducible JSON. No alternative is credited with free capital or a demonstrated$1 paying customer.

## Why this experiment

OBSERVE: frozen capacity work transferred funded CKB rights but did not exchange two assets. WHY: its state lacked authenticated second-asset liability and reserve pairing. IF authentic xUDT inventory backs that liability, owners might exchange without personal funding Cells. ELSE an internal balance would merely re-label an unfunded promise. WHAT IF direct wallets and A2 already do it better? Execute all three with identical asset quantities and quoted price.

RESULT: native two-asset exchange and eventual delivery succeed, but direct wallets/published A2 use fewer cycles and A2 also accepts third-party-funded creation. REDESIGN DECISION: preserve pool as optional repeated-trading/storage layer; do not replace A2/direct settlement or advertise a new execution advantage. Next decisive question is access to genuine quoted asset inventory and sustainable replenishment, followed by whether a thin adapter can use established makers without forcing them into a custom pool. No additional storage scheme is justified.

## Evidence versus hypotheses

VERIFIED: bounded new native TEST-xUDT/CKB lifecycle, real signatures, captured rival transactions and public dated market responses. DERIVED: partial amortization and maker-loss counterexamples. HYPOTHESIS: native-asset repeat users would value shared storage enough to cover archive/custom-contract costs. MISSING: paying users, actual native two-way executable inventory quotes, replenishment costs, end-to-end competitive security qualification, equivalent live CoBuild/AMM/Fiber routes and unconditional small external exit.

No mainnet transactions, customer contacts, solicitations, external commitments or paid infrastructure were used. No claim that all public protocols lack liquidity is supported merely because a quote was not obtained here.
